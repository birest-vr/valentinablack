Behind the Song

Deep Sadness for the World

The birth of "Say You Love the World"

Published September 26, 2026
Behind the SongSay You Love The WorldSongwriting

"Say You Love the World" was born out of one of Valentina Black's moments of solitude and introspection, shortly before the Covid pandemic began. She could not explain the sadness that had settled over her. Nothing recent had happened to cause it — no loss, no specific pain she could point to — and yet she found she could not remain indifferent to the feeling. So she went to her studio and started talking through the only instrument that seemed able to listen: her favourite microphone, a Shure SM7.

Sounds That Arrive Already Finished

She began improvising, letting sounds come without deciding them in advance. Valentina describes it as a belief she holds about how songs are made: that they arrive already finished, as messages sent into our physical dimension, and that it is the brain's task to translate them into the "matter" of human, collective communication — something that can be shared. She was not writing a song that night, in her account of it. She was receiving one.

Out of that improvisation, the words came:

"Your love is nothing without your real love for this world,
Why don't you love the world more?
Say you love the world, say you love the world"

From there, everything else followed in the same unplanned order: the bass line, the keys, the choral layers, the rhythm underneath it all.

A Song That Has to Finish Itself

Valentina usually composes this way — improvising, in the moment, rather than sitting down to construct a song piece by piece. She says a song has to complete itself entirely on its own, without effort and without mechanical intention. If it doesn't, she believes the intention behind it becomes impure, or commercial. Music itself, she's quick to add, can be used by anyone for any intention at all — but "Say You Love the World" was never meant to be one of those songs. It was meant to finish the way it started: on its own.
